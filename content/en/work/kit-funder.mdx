## Context

Grassroots teams pay for kit, travel and pitch fees every season, and most of them find sponsors through whoever happens to know a local business owner. Businesses that want to support their community have the opposite problem: no simple way to find a team that fits their values, budget and area. Kit Funder puts both sides in one place and does the introductions.

## Constraints

- **Trust on both sides.** Clubs include youth teams, so contact details and photos stay hidden until both sides say yes.
- **Explainable matches.** A score on its own does not persuade a business to spend money; every match needed a reason in plain language.
- **No fees for clubs.** It is a not-for-profit, so the running cost had to stay low and the stack had to be maintainable by a small team.

## My role

I led the build: the data model, the matching engine, the backend functions for scoring, messaging and email, the CRM sync, and most of the app's flows. I worked with an AI app builder for the routine UI scaffolding and wrote and reviewed the logic myself. The marketing site is a separate Webflow build.

## Architecture

- **App**: a React and Vite single-page app on Base44, with TanStack Query, react-hook-form and Zod, Recharts for the compatibility radar, Leaflet for maps and jsPDF for proposals.
- **Backend**: Base44 entities plus Deno serverless functions for match scoring, notifications, geocoding (UK postcodes) and proposal handling.
- **Matching**: a batch job scores every eligible club–sponsor pair on nine weighted dimensions: location, sports, budget, demographics, CSR focus, sector, organisation type, activation preferences, and an LLM judgement of overall fit. Some combinations are blocked before scoring.
- **Integrations**: Resend for email, and an n8n webhook that keeps the CRM in step with sign-ups, matches and deals.
- **Marketing site**: kitfunder.ai on Webflow, separate from the app at app.kitfunder.ai.

## Three decisions

**1. Rules first, the model for judgement.** Eight of the nine dimensions are deterministic, so most of the score is predictable and testable. The LLM covers what rules cannot, such as whether a company's story fits a team's, and writes the explanation. If the model call fails, the other weights are re-normalised so scores stay on the same 0–100 scale.

**2. Mutual opt-in before any contact.** Matches are anonymous until both sides accept. That one rule removed most of the safeguarding questions and made businesses more willing to browse.

**3. Keep the CRM in the loop from day one.** Every meaningful event goes out through a webhook, so the team can follow up with clubs and sponsors from the tools they already use instead of a separate admin panel.

## Outcome

- Live at kitfunder.ai, with clubs and businesses signing up and matching.
- Every match comes with a score breakdown and a written reason, which is what businesses respond to.
- A full path from sign-up to an agreed sponsorship inside one product: matches, messaging, a kit cost calculator and proposals.

## What I'd do differently

Separate the scoring engine into its own tested module earlier. It grew inside a serverless function, which made it harder to replay old matches after a weight change. I would also log which explanations led to a connection, to tune the LLM prompt against real outcomes rather than taste.
