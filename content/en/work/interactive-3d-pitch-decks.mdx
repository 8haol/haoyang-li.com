## Context

Enterprise proposals were PDF slides: easy to skim and forget. For a handful of large prospects, the team wanted something that made people stop and scroll, and that could be walked through live on a call or left behind afterwards.

## Constraints

- **Works anywhere.** Decks are opened from email, on locked-down corporate laptops, sometimes offline. No server, no install.
- **Fast to adapt.** Each prospect needed its own story, brand and numbers, without rebuilding from scratch.
- **Content first.** Motion had to support the argument, not hide it; every section still reads as a normal page.

## My role

I designed the template, built the 3D scenes and the scroll system, wrote the build tooling, and produced each deck with the sales team's content.

## Architecture

- **Template**: one HTML page with sections for the opportunity, the model, the platform, the range, pricing, timeline and next steps.
- **3D**: a Three.js centrepiece (a lit globe or a ball) that reacts to scroll.
- **Motion**: Lenis for smooth scrolling, GSAP ScrollTrigger for pinned and scrubbed sections.
- **Build**: a Python script inlines fonts, images, scripts and media into a single self-contained file, so a deck opens by double-click.
- **Theming**: brand colour and logos are variables, so a re-skin is mostly content.

## Three decisions

**1. One file, no server.** Inlining everything makes decks large but bulletproof: they survive email, firewalls and conference Wi-Fi.

**2. A template, not a framework.** A plain page with clear sections was faster for the sales team to edit than a component system would have been.

**3. One 3D object, done well.** A single scene that responds to scroll carries the "this is different" moment; the rest of the page stays quiet and readable.

## Outcome

- Five decks produced and used in live enterprise pitches.
- Decks open offline, anywhere, from a single file.
- New decks start from the template, so the effort goes into the story rather than the build.

## What I'd do differently

Pull the copy out into a data file per prospect from the first deck. Editing text inside a large HTML template was the slowest and most error-prone part of each new version.

## Stack

Three.js, GSAP, ScrollTrigger, Lenis, HTML and CSS, Python build script, Netlify for hosted versions.
