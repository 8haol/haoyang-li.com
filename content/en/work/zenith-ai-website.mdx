## Context

Zenith AI sells AI systems, web builds, integration and growth work. Its first site was assembled quickly on a low-code builder and looked like every other agency site. The new one had to show, in the first few seconds, that the team builds things with care.

## Constraints

- **Motion without lag.** The site leans on scroll animation and WebGL; it still had to feel instant on a mid-range laptop and a phone.
- **Readable first.** Visitors are business owners, not designers. Every section had to say plainly what Zenith does before it showed off.
- **One person.** I designed it, wrote most of the copy structure and built it.

## My role

Everything: art direction, layout, the motion system, the Three.js scenes, content structure, the contact pipeline and deployment.

## Architecture

- **Next.js 15 and React 19**, statically rendered where possible.
- **Lenis** for smooth scrolling and **GSAP ScrollTrigger** for pinned and scrubbed sections, sharing one clock so they never drift apart.
- **Three.js** loaded on demand, only on the routes that use it, so the first paint never waits for a 3D bundle.
- **Contact** form posting to a route that sends through Resend and optionally forwards to the CRM by webhook.
- **CI** on GitHub Actions for lint, types and build; served behind Cloudflare.

## Three decisions

**1. One metaphor, carried through.** The site is an ascent: altitude markers, a hero that climbs, sections as stages. A single idea gave every design choice a test to pass.

**2. Pay for 3D only where it earns it.** The WebGL is code-split per route and starts after the text is on screen. The page is readable before any of it loads.

**3. Motion follows the scroll, not timers.** Animations are scrubbed by scroll position, so they never play out of sync with what the reader is looking at, and they degrade to static layouts under reduced motion.

## Outcome

- Live at zenithai.one, replacing the earlier low-code version.
- A site that shows the build quality the team sells instead of describing it.
- Lightweight to maintain: a single repository with CI, no CMS to babysit.

## What I'd do differently

Set the performance budget in CI from the first commit. An automatic check on bundle size and long tasks catches a heavy dependency the day it lands, not in a manual pass at the end.
