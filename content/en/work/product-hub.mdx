## Context

New products moved through product development, finance, production and sales by email and shared spreadsheets. Nobody could say where a product was, which version of a spec had been approved, or who was holding it up.

## Constraints

- **Documents stay where people work.** Teams live in Word and Excel on SharePoint; asking them to upload files into another tool would have failed.
- **Approvals must be provable.** An approval had to point at the exact version that was approved, even after the file changed.
- **Different departments, different rights.** Finance approves prices, production approves samples; everyone can see progress.

## My role

I gathered the process from each department, designed the data model and permissions, and built the app and its integrations.

## Architecture

- **App**: React on Base44, with 27 entities covering products, stages, documents, price books, samples and requests.
- **Pipeline**: stage templates generate each product's critical path; a kanban shows every product's stage.
- **Documents**: Microsoft Graph creates and reads files in SharePoint; approving a document stores a frozen snapshot.
- **Permissions**: grants per department (edit or approve) plus company-level approval, resolved in one tested function.
- **Prices**: governed change requests with a dry-run diff and rollback.
- **Digest**: a scheduled job at 08:00 on weekdays sends each person what is waiting on them.
- **Integrations**: HMAC-signed events out to n8n and verified callbacks in, with the workflows handed to the tech team.

## Three decisions

**1. Link documents, do not copy them.** The hub stores references and snapshots, not uploads. People keep editing in Word; the hub always shows the current file and the approved one.

**2. Grants, not roles.** Departments needed different rights on different stages. A grant table per department was simpler to reason about, and to test, than a growing list of roles.

**3. Push the work to people.** The morning digest does more for adoption than any dashboard: nobody has to remember to open the app.

## Outcome

- Every product's stage, documents and approvals in one place.
- Approvals point at the exact document version that was signed.
- Each department starts the day with a list of what is waiting on it.

## What I'd do differently

Pilot with one department before building for all of them. Real usage shows which approvals matter, and the permission model should follow that rather than lead it.

## Stack

React, Vite, Base44 with Deno functions, Microsoft Graph (SharePoint, Word, Excel), n8n, Vitest.
